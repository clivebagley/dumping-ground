Canalside Garden Map

A plan of the plot from the street to the canal, with what is growing in each zone and how sure the identification is. Drawn from your satellite view, photos, video stills and corrections, last refreshed on 6 November 2026. Full care guidance, month by month with sources, is in the separate care plan document.

Zones, street to canal

Before the first frost

  • Move the begonias and the Jerusalem cherry to bright, frost-free shelter. The aloe and echeveria are already indoors.
  • Bin black-spotted and fallen rose leaves (standard rose, rose in the cordyline pot).
  • Pin wire mesh over the bulb pots; a bulb has already been dug up.

December to February

  • Untangle the kiwi from the cordylines, finishing by the end of January. Keep the framework on the trellis.
  • Renovate the old pergola rose once it is leafless: cut a few old stems low, keep up to six strong ones tied in.
  • Trim iris fans to about 15 cm, keep bark off the rhizomes, and cut dead hollyhock stems to the ground.
  • Leave the hydrangea alone until February or March: next summer's flower buds are already on it.
  • Cut old hellebore leaves in January or February.
  • Prune the two standard roses in February or March and fleece the graft unions in hard frost.
  • Cut the Miscanthus to 10 to 15 cm in February or March.

March to April

  • Cut the grafted willow standard hard, to two or three buds.
  • Trim the Photinia-type standards.
  • Prune the Kilmarnock willow to 10 to 15 cm once the catkins finish.
  • Look for new lupin shoots; cut back verbena and fuchsia; cut the Artemisia back by half in April.
  • Fleece the kiwi and wisteria shoots if a late frost threatens.

Spring checks that settle identifications

  • Leaf shape on the two maples (garage wall, by the Fatsia) and blossom on the front tree.
  • Scratch test the broom. Crush a leaf of the silver plant, and of the lemon balm in spring.
  • Flowers on the rock-bed cushions, and on the bulbs.
